Why the same grade can hide different problems

A grade tells you where a student is, not why they are there.

Two students both working at Grade 3 can need very different support. One may need number fluency. Another may know methods but freeze when choosing independently.

Some students can perform isolated skills but cannot connect them in a longer question. Others start correctly, then lose the final steps because the wording, layout or method choice becomes unclear.

Good GCSE support starts by finding the reason behind the grade, then building from the missing step instead of giving every student the same intervention.

What might be holding progress back

The barrier is often smaller and more specific than it feels.

Around Grades 3, 4 and 5, progress can stall for reasons that are easy to miss if you only look at the final mark.

  • Weak number fluency
  • Knowing methods but not choosing them independently
  • Struggling with multi-step questions
  • Misreading mathematical wording
  • Starting correctly but not finishing
  • Performing isolated skills but not connecting them

Grade 4 to Grade 5 support

Turn partial understanding into more independent exam work.

Moving from Grade 4 towards Grade 5 is often about connecting skills, interpreting questions more accurately, and finishing methods under exam conditions.

Support stays targeted. A student who misreads ratio questions does not need the same lesson as a student who knows ratio but loses accuracy in multi-step working.

  • Interpreting GCSE wording
  • Choosing methods independently
  • Connecting topics in longer questions
  • Checking whether an answer makes sense
  • Finishing multi-step working
  • Building exam habits without panic

How the diagnostic approach works

Find the pattern before choosing the support.

The diagnostic approach looks at actual working, not just final answers. It asks what broke down, when it broke down, and what the student was trying to do at that point.

Look at real working

Mistakes are explored line by line so the cause is clearer than a topic label.

Separate skill from confidence

Sometimes the maths is nearly there, but exam habits or confidence stop it showing.

Prioritise the first gap

Support starts with the step that will make later work easier, not with random worksheet practice.

Review progress carefully

As a student becomes more secure, the plan can shift towards application, reasoning and exam independence.

Missing Step approach

Start from the exact point where understanding breaks.

A student might know the formula, understand the first line, or get the arithmetic right, but still lose the mark because one connection is missing.

The Missing Step approach slows that moment down. It finds the first uncertain decision, rebuilds it clearly, then practises it in different question types until the student can use it more independently.

That keeps support personal. It avoids assuming that every Grade 3, 4 or 5 student needs the same topic list or the same pace.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about Grade 4 and 5 GCSE Maths support.

Support is personalised, and I do not guarantee a particular grade. The focus is on identifying what is holding progress back and building from there.

Is this only for students currently at Grade 3?

No. It is for students working around Grades 3, 4 and 5, including students trying to secure a pass or become more confident beyond a Grade 4.

Can you guarantee a Grade 4 or 5?

No. The support is designed to identify gaps, rebuild missing steps and improve exam habits, but no grade outcome can be guaranteed.

What if my child knows topics in class but freezes in exams?

That can happen when method choice, confidence, wording or timing gets in the way. Support looks at those habits as well as the maths content.

Is this suitable for Foundation and Higher?

Yes. Hull Maths Tutor supports GCSE Foundation and Higher, and the plan depends on the student’s current level, gaps and goals.

Should we start with the Maths MOT?

It can be a useful first step if you are not sure why marks are being lost. The Maths MOT gives diagnostic insight before deciding what support should come next.
