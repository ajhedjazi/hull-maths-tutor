What is the Maths MOT?

A diagnostic GCSE Maths assessment, not just more questions.

The Maths MOT is a focused GCSE Maths gaps assessment for students in Hull who need more than a score or a list of weak topics.

During the session, Amir looks at how your child approaches maths: what is secure, what is nearly there, where misconceptions appear, and which missing connections are making questions feel harder than they should.

The aim is to understand patterns in working, confidence and method choice, then turn that into clear next steps for parents and students.

Who it helps

Useful when effort is not turning into marks.

The Maths MOT is for students who are trying, revising, or attending lessons, but still cannot see exactly why marks are being lost.

  • Students who revise but still get stuck
  • Students who lose marks without knowing why
  • Students who say, “I can do it in class but not in the exam”
  • Students who struggle with multi-step GCSE questions
  • Students who lack confidence or avoid starting questions
  • Students working around the Grade 3-5 boundary
  • Post-16 resit students who need a clearer route forward

If your child is hovering around that boundary, you may also find the page on Grade 4 & 5 GCSE Maths support useful.

Diagnostic depth

What I look for during the assessment.

The point is not to label a student as good or bad at maths. It is to find the exact place where thinking becomes uncertain.

That means looking beyond final answers and paying attention to the working, the language, the confidence and the decisions made during a question.

  • Secure methods
  • Arithmetic fluency
  • Misconceptions
  • Mathematical vocabulary
  • Question interpretation
  • Confidence
  • Starting a question but failing to finish
  • Connecting several skills
  • Layout and working
  • Recall versus application
  • Method selection
  • Multi-step reasoning

Frequently asked questions

Questions parents often ask.

Is this a normal tutoring lesson?

No. The Maths MOT is a diagnostic first step. It looks at how your child is thinking, where marks are being lost, and what support would make most sense next.

Does my child need to prepare?

No special preparation is needed. The point is to see their current working, confidence and approach as they are now.

Is it suitable for Foundation and Higher?

Yes. Hull Maths Tutor supports GCSE Foundation and Higher, and the MOT is shaped around the student’s current level, gaps and goals.

What happens after the MOT?

Parents receive clear feedback and a personalised action plan. You can then decide whether regular tuition, an intensive session, or no further lessons is the right next step.

Do I have to commit to regular tutoring?

No. There is no obligation to continue with regular tutoring after the Maths MOT & Action Plan.

Is it available online or in person?

The Maths MOT & Action Plan is described as an in-person diagnostic assessment in Hull. Regular one-to-one GCSE maths tuition can also be arranged online where appropriate.

Is it suitable for post-16 resit students?

Yes. Hull Maths Tutor focuses on GCSE maths, including post-16 students preparing for a resit.
